# KATARI Osaka Story Spot Sources

This registry is the editorial source of truth for KATARI's 20-spot Osaka MVP. It is not runtime prose. Institutional and primary sources take precedence in this order: place or collection owner, public authority, heritage or university archive, transport operator, then official destination organisation. A story claim must remain supportable after removing promotional language.

KATARI paraphrases facts; it does not copy source prose or reuse source images. `Verified` records the date on which the linked page and the stated claim were checked, not a promise that a page will remain unchanged. Coordinates define a candidate area only and never establish identity without corroborating place or visual evidence. `fact`, `tradition`, and `legend` are separate knowledge kinds; a traditional account is never upgraded into a documented event.

All 20 ordered candidates passed the central-story gate on 2026-09-09. No reserve candidate was required. The socially popular nickname `ひっかけ橋` was rejected as an Ebisu Bridge story because the selected official sources do not establish one reliable origin.

## Spot: dotonbori

- Place JA: 道頓堀
- Place EN: Dotonbori
- Category: district
- Locality: Dotonbori, Chuo Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6687, 135.5020
- Candidate radius: 300 m
- Visual anchors: Dotonbori canal; dense illuminated signs; riverside promenade; theatre and restaurant facades
- Facts:
  1. Osaka's official tourism organisation describes Dotonbori as a representative entertainment district of Minami.
  2. The south bank developed as a theatre quarter after the canal was opened.
  3. Food businesses grew around audiences attending the theatres.
- Story claim: Dotonbori's restaurant spectacle grew from an older theatre district where businesses gathered to serve audiences.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://osaka-info.jp/spot/gastronomy-doutonbori/
  - https://www.city.osaka.lg.jp/chuo/cmsfiles/contents/0000508/508145/vol.10ura.pdf
- Source classes: Osaka Convention and Tourism Bureau; Osaka City local-history publication
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The canal's naming and completion history is more complex than this one story, so the claim is limited to the documented theatre-to-dining relationship.

## Spot: ebisu-bridge

- Place JA: 戎橋
- Place EN: Ebisu Bridge
- Category: bridge
- Locality: Dotonbori, Chuo Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6687, 135.5013
- Candidate radius: 80 m
- Visual anchors: oval bridge deck; stainless-steel balustrade; Dotonbori canal; adjacent large signs
- Facts:
  1. Osaka City says the bridge was likely built around the same time as the Dotonbori canal.
  2. The bridge served people heading toward Imamiya Ebisu and the theatres south of it.
  3. It was also called Ayatsuri Bridge because a puppet theatre stood on its south side.
- Story claim: Before it became a sign-viewing landmark, the bridge carried worshippers and theatre-goers through Osaka's entertainment district.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://www.city.osaka.lg.jp/kensetsu/page/0000021695.html
  - https://www.ebisubashi.or.jp/sp/ja/about.html
- Source classes: Osaka City bridge history; Ebisubashi shopping district history
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: Osaka City says the bridge-name origin is not certain, so the story does not assert one origin as fact.

## Spot: hozenji-yokocho

- Place JA: 法善寺横丁
- Place EN: Hozenji Yokocho
- Category: alley
- Locality: Namba, Chuo Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6675, 135.5023
- Candidate radius: 75 m
- Visual anchors: narrow stone-paved lanes; lanterns; small restaurant fronts; east and west gateway signs
- Facts:
  1. Hozenji says the alley began with stalls serving worshippers in the temple grounds during the middle Edo period.
  2. The two lanes are each less than three metres wide and retain a compact streetscape.
  3. After fires in 2002 and 2003, more than 300,000 supporting signatures helped restoration retain the earlier atmosphere.
- Story claim: The alley kept its narrow character after two fires because residents, artists, and visitors rallied to preserve the place they remembered.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://www.houzenji.jp/
- Source classes: Hozenji temple official history
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The source reports the signature total as more than 300,000; KATARI does not infer how each signature affected the legal restoration process.

## Spot: mizukake-fudo

- Place JA: 水掛不動尊
- Place EN: Mizukake Fudo
- Category: religious object
- Locality: Hozenji, Chuo Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6676, 135.5025
- Candidate radius: 35 m
- Visual anchors: moss-covered standing figure; water ladles; stone basin; incense and temple lanterns
- Facts:
  1. Hozenji identifies the figure as the west-facing Fudo Myoo known as Mizukake Fudo.
  2. The temple says the water-pouring custom began only after the war, despite Hozenji's much longer history.
  3. Its official account traces the custom to a woman who scooped offered water and poured it over the figure while praying.
- Story claim: The thick moss looks ancient, but the water ritual that feeds it began after the war with one worshipper's spontaneous act.
- Knowledge kind: tradition
- Sources:
  - https://houzenji.jp/
- Source classes: Hozenji temple official worship tradition
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The initiating worshipper is part of the temple's transmitted account and is therefore labelled tradition, not independently documented fact.

## Spot: osaka-castle

- Place JA: 大阪城
- Place EN: Osaka Castle
- Category: castle
- Locality: Osaka Castle Park, Chuo Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6873, 135.5262
- Candidate radius: 300 m
- Visual anchors: white and green main tower; massive stone walls; moats; gold ornament
- Facts:
  1. Osaka Castle's museum says the visible main tower is the third generation on the site.
  2. Citizens' donations funded the present reinforced-concrete tower, completed in 1931.
  3. Most of the stone walls and moats visible today belong to the later Tokugawa reconstruction, not the buried Toyotomi castle.
- Story claim: The familiar tower is neither the Toyotomi original nor a feudal survival; Osaka citizens paid to raise this third-generation landmark.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://www.osakacastle.net/?lang=ja
  - https://www.osakacastle.net/toyotomi_stone_wall/index.html
- Source classes: Osaka Castle Museum; Osaka Castle Toyotomi Stone Wall Museum
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: This spot covers the main tower view; identification of a particular wall or buried structure requires a more specific location than the MVP provides.

## Spot: tsutenkaku

- Place JA: 通天閣
- Place EN: Tsutenkaku Tower
- Category: tower
- Locality: Shinsekai, Naniwa Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6525, 135.5063
- Candidate radius: 110 m
- Visual anchors: latticed observation tower; vertical 通天閣 sign; clock face; Shinsekai shop canopies below
- Facts:
  1. The first Tsutenkaku was built with Luna Park in 1912.
  2. Its design combined forms associated with Paris's Arc de Triomphe and Eiffel Tower.
  3. After a fire at the cinema below, the first tower was dismantled in 1943; the current second tower opened in 1956.
- Story claim: Osaka once lost this skyline symbol after a fire, and Shinsekai's local community organised to bring a second tower back.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://tsutenkaku.co.jp/other/shiryo.html
  - https://www.tsutenkaku.co.jp/Guide-pdf/mishiranguide_japanese.pdf
- Source classes: Tsutenkaku official archive and guide
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The story says the first tower was dismantled after the fire and does not compress later wartime material use into the cause of the fire.

## Spot: shinsekai

- Place EN: Shinsekai
- Category: district
- Locality: Ebisuhigashi, Naniwa Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6521, 135.5061

- Facts:

- Story claim: The name New World was once literal marketing: this Osaka neighbourhood was staged as a dream of Paris and New York.

- Source classes: Osaka City Naniwa Ward history
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: Shinsekai is a broad district; the Page must avoid claiming that every street feature dates from the original Luna Park plan.

- Facts:

- Story claim: The black gate is gone, but its colour survived as the market's name and became a piece of city memory.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://www.city.osaka.lg.jp/higashisumiyoshi/page/0000032783.html

- Source classes: Osaka City cultural property note; Osaka Prefecture shopping-street portal
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The market spans several entrances, so a generic covered arcade without a Kuromon sign is not sufficient visual evidence.

## Spot: shinsaibashi-suji

- Place JA: 心斎橋筋
- Place EN: Shinsaibashi-suji
- Category: shopping street
- Locality: Shinsaibashi, Chuo Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6744, 135.5016
- Candidate radius: 300 m
- Visual anchors: long covered arcade; 心斎橋筋 gateway lettering; dense storefronts; patterned pavement
- Facts:
  1. The shopping street's official history links the name Shinsaibashi to Okada Shinsai, who helped develop the Nagahori canal.
  2. A bridge was built here around 1622 as the district grew.
  3. Stone parts from an older bridge were later reused as railings above the present underground shopping area.
- Story claim: The bridge that gave Shinsaibashi its name disappeared from street level, yet pieces of an older bridge still remain above the underground mall.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://www.shinsaibashi.or.jp/histories/his_chronology/
- Source classes: Shinsaibashi-suji shopping district official chronology
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The spot is a long arcade; the retained bridge stones are a memory hook, not a claim that they are visible from every position.

## Spot: shitennoji

- Place JA: 四天王寺
- Place EN: Shitennoji Temple
- Category: temple
- Locality: Shitennoji, Tennoji Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6546, 135.5165
- Candidate radius: 260 m
- Visual anchors: five-storey pagoda; aligned central gate and golden hall; stone torii; broad temple precinct
- Facts:
  1. Shitennoji presents its foundation through an account recorded in the Nihon Shoki.
  2. In that account, Prince Shotoku vowed to enshrine the Four Heavenly Kings and founded the temple in 593.
  3. The temple also describes four institutions for religion, medicine, welfare, and education associated with its founding ideal.
- Story claim: Shitennoji remembers its beginning not only as a temple foundation, but as a plan to care for bodies, learning, and people in need.
- Knowledge kind: tradition
- Sources:
  - https://www.shitennoji.or.jp/history.html
- Source classes: Shitennoji official history citing the Nihon Shoki
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The foundation narrative is an ancient textual tradition, so KATARI attributes it to the temple account rather than treating every detail as contemporary evidence.

- Locality: Tenjinbashi, Kita Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6960, 135.5123

- Visual anchors: stone torii; white lantern curtains; main sanctuary; plum crest
- Facts:
  1. Osaka Tenmangu says an older Daishogun shrine already stood on the site before Tenmangu was founded.
  2. Its official origin account says seven pines shone at night in 949 and prompted an imperial order to establish the shrine.
  3. The shrine still performs a year-end rite described as paying symbolic rent to Daishogun for the ground.
- Story claim: Even a major shrine remembers that it arrived second: each year it symbolically pays rent to the older shrine already on the ground.
- Knowledge kind: tradition
- Sources:
  - https://osakatemmangu.or.jp/about
- Source classes: Osaka Tenmangu official origin and ritual history
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The glowing-pine origin is a shrine tradition; the runtime story focuses on the shrine's documented continuing interpretation of the rent rite.

## Spot: osaka-central-public-hall

- Place JA: 大阪市中央公会堂
- Place EN: Osaka City Central Public Hall
- Category: civic building
- Locality: Nakanoshima, Kita Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6933, 135.5050
- Candidate radius: 100 m
- Visual anchors: red-brick facade; pale stone bands; green copper roof; twin rounded corner towers
- Facts:
  1. Construction was funded substantially by a donation from Osaka stockbroker Einosuke Iwamoto.
  2. The public hall opened in 1918 and hosted cultural and civic events.
  3. When demolition was considered, a citizen preservation movement and red-brick fund supported restoration; the renewed hall reopened in 2002.
- Story claim: This grand public building exists twice because citizens first received it through a private gift, then later helped save its red bricks from demolition.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://osaka-chuokokaido.jp/news/riyou_annai.pdf
- Source classes: Osaka City Central Public Hall official facility history
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The story compresses a multi-year preservation and restoration process without claiming that donations alone determined every administrative decision.

## Spot: tekijuku

- Place JA: 適塾
- Place EN: Tekijuku
- Category: historic school
- Locality: Kitahama, Chuo Ward, Osaka
- Coordinates: 34.6917, 135.5043

- Visual anchors: low traditional townhouse; dark timber lattice; white plaster; stone Tekijuku marker
- Facts:
  1. Osaka University says physician Koan Ogata opened Tekijuku in 1838 as a school of Dutch studies.
  2. The surviving townhouse is the only remaining building of a Dutch-studies school in Japan.
  3. The building survived all eight major Osaka air raids and later became part of Osaka University's institutional heritage.
- Story claim: This modest townhouse survived eight major air raids, carrying a line of medical learning into the university that now preserves it.
- Knowledge kind: fact
- Sources:
  - https://www.tekijuku.osaka-u.ac.jp/ja/tekijuku

- Source classes: Osaka University Tekijuku Commemoration Center
- Verified: 2026-09-09
- Uncertainty: The lineage to Osaka University is institutional history, not a claim that the present university existed in the school's original form.
